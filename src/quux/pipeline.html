THE MACHINES · QUUX · REVISION 15

Four stages,
one clock each.

Each microinstruction takes four stages of one clock each. Data moves left to right; control decisions flow back to the fetch.

This is the design muir-sim’s rtl models and the FPGA core matches clock for clock.

Revision 15 is QUUX’s next revision, in development and not released. System 2002 will run on it; today’s released QUUX is revision 13, with System 2001.

The four stages are CS, fetch; RD, read and decide; EX, execute and check; and WB, write and start. Below the figure, six notes give what a wrong guess costs, why predictions pay, the fused dispatch D, the memory, halt and checkpoint, and the clock today.

02 / NOTES

Six notes.

What a wrong guess costs

A wrong prediction squashes the words behind the delay slot and restores RD’s copies: 1 bubble. A start that page-faults squashes late: 2 bubbles.

Why predictions pay

The profile pass sets each jump’s hint and each dispatch’s predicted target from the measured majority. On the twelve workloads it cuts the clocks by about 8%.

D, the fused dispatch

A main-loop return can fetch the next macroinstruction and dispatch on it. Its effects wait for the fetched word and start the handler in CS one clock later (L = 1). Forced on, it saves about 8% of the clocks; the microcode turns it on next.

Memory

Two different caches sit on the way: the TLB holds address translations (a virtual page to a physical frame), and the data cache holds memory words (64K on the Kria, 16K on the DE25, 4K on the Arty). Writes are posted: WB hands them to the queue and goes on. A word written is fixed when its start’s microcycle ends. A read waits only when its line has a write still on its way.

Halt and checkpoint

A halt completes the words in EX and WB, squashes CS and RD, and waits for the port to empty. The machine then stands between two microcycles, as the single-edge simulator would.

The clock today

The routed core closes at about 13 ns on the Kria. Its longest path runs from EX’s decision back into the next fetch. Timing work comes later; the target is about 11 ns.
