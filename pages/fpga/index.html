01 / WHAT IT IS

A CADR
in the fabric.

muir-fpga puts QUUX and the CADR in the fabric of an FPGA. The CADR is the Lisp Machine MIT designed around 1978, and muir-fpga’s CADR is muir-sim’s RTL model of it, synthesized into the fabric of an FPGA — the two processor boards, the bus interface, the disk controller, the display and the I/O board.

Its clock edges are close to the CADR’s but not identical. The CADR placed them with delay lines, and the FPGA can place them only on the ticks of one 10 ns clock, so some fall up to 7 ns later. The timing, instant by instant.

The same fabric is built as QUUX too, the CADR evolved. QUUX runs on the Arty Z7-20 and the DE25-Nano, and the Cora Z7-07S and the Kria KR260 run the CADR. The CADR boots MIT’s own system software; QUUX’s system is muir-sys’s updated one. The CADR gets its files and the time from ozd, the CADR’s file and time host. QUUX does not need ozd, having a file device and a real-time clock of its own.

QUUX is still being developed: its hardware revisions, microcode and system change, and a later QUUX need not run today’s bands or microcode.

05 / ABOUT

MIT’s machine, and
what muir-fpga adds.

The CADR, the schematics, the wire lists and the microcode are MIT’s, recovered by other people’s work over decades, and the machine in the fabric is held tick for tick to muir-sim; the documents list every source the drawings were read from and what each one is. The fabric, its checks and their reference traces, the programs beside the machine on the board and the documents are this project’s.

muir-fpga is written with Claude Code, using Anthropic’s Claude Opus, Claude Fable and Claude Sonnet. The machine is written in SystemVerilog, its testbenches in C++ for Verilator, the programs beside it on the board in C, and the generators of its reference traces in Rust.

muir-fpga is muir-sim’s RTL model of the CADR and of QUUX, synthesized into the fabric of an FPGA and held to muir-sim tick for tick.
